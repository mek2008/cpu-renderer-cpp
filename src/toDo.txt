V3.1 PROPOSED FIX: REFLECTION SPREAD AT GRAZING ANGLES

Status: Proposed only, not implemented or verified Target: Renderer
v3.1

Problem

Reflection rays are sampled around the ideal reflection direction using
a spread value. At grazing angles, some of the perturbed sample
directions may point back through the surface that generated them.

This can cause self-intersections and unwanted secondary reflections. It
may also make reflections appear weaker or behave inconsistently at
steep viewing angles if invalid samples are rejected without correcting
the sample average.

Proposed approach

Investigate constraining the reflection spread so that the sampled
directions remain on the reflective side of the triangle’s surface.

The triangle can be treated as an infinite plane defined by:

    P0 = hit.cord
    N  = hit.normal

A candidate ray is:

    R(t) = O + tD

Its intersection parameter with the plane is:

    t = dot(P0 - O, N) / dot(D, N)

A near-zero denominator means the ray is parallel (or nearly parallel)
to the plane. A positive t means the plane intersection is in front of
the ray origin; a negative t means it is behind the origin.

Possible implementation direction

1.  Calculate the ideal reflection direction.
2.  Construct candidate directions representing the boundary/extremes of
    the reflection sampling region.
3.  Test those candidates against the triangle’s infinite plane.
4.  If the spread would send samples through the plane, reduce or
    otherwise constrain the spread before generating the normal
    reflection samples.

Important: this is only a proposed direction, not a proven algorithm.
The sampling region is two-dimensional (it uses two independent basis
vectors), so checking only two candidate directions may not cover every
edge or corner of the sampling region. The correct boundary candidates
must be derived from the actual sampling basis.

Further work required

-   Re-derive the geometry carefully and verify the cross-product basis.
-   Determine which candidate directions bound the full two-dimensional
    sampling region, including its corners.
-   Handle nearly parallel rays and degenerate projections safely.
-   Account for the reflection ray’s origin offset when evaluating t.
-   Decide how the spread should be adjusted when a candidate crosses
    the plane (for example, reducing it to the largest valid spread).
-   Verify that the adjustment does not unintentionally change
    reflection strength or create visible discontinuities as the viewing
    angle changes.
-   Test at normal incidence, grazing incidence, multiple spread values,
    and different triangle orientations before considering the fix
    complete.

Do not copy the earlier experimental check directly into the renderer.
Its cross-product construction and choice of extreme directions were not
fully validated, and it did not establish that the entire sampling
region was safe.

Decision

Defer this improvement to v3.1. Keep the current reflection sampling for
now. Revisit only after the geometry is understood and the proposed
constraint has been tested independently.
